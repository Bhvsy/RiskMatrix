  1. Enter Your Account Balance

    Input the total funded account balance provided by your prop firm. Use the slider or type the exact value. Quick presets are available for common account sizes.

    Configure your prop firm's daily and maximum drawdown rules. Use the preset buttons for industry-standard configurations, or enter custom values for your specific firm.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is drawdown in prop firm trading?

Drawdown is the maximum loss a trader is allowed to incur on a funded account before the account is breached. Prop firms enforce two types: Daily Drawdown (max loss in a single day) and Maximum Drawdown (total cumulative loss from initial balance or highest equity). Exceeding either limit results in account termination.

How is lot size calculated for prop firm compliance?

Lot size is calculated by dividing the dollar amount you are willing to risk per trade by the pip/point value of your stop loss. The formula is: Lot Size = (Account Balance × Risk %) ÷ (Stop Loss in Pips × Pip Value per Standard Lot). This ensures each trade risks only a controlled fraction of your account and keeps you within drawdown limits.

What is the difference between daily and maximum drawdown?

Daily drawdown is the maximum loss permitted in a single trading day, typically calculated from the start-of-day equity or balance. Maximum drawdown is the total cumulative loss allowed from the initial account balance or the highest recorded equity point. Both are independent limits — breaching either one results in account failure.

How much should I risk per trade on a funded account?

Most professional funded traders risk between 0.5% and 2% of their account balance per trade. Conservative risk (0.5–1%) provides more room for consecutive losses and is recommended for newer funded traders. The ideal risk depends on your win rate, average R:R ratio, and how close you are to your drawdown limits.

What happens if I exceed my drawdown limit?

If you exceed either your daily or maximum drawdown limit, most prop firms will automatically close all open positions and terminate or suspend your funded account. You would typically need to purchase a new challenge or evaluation to regain a funded account. Some firms offer reset options at a discounted fee.
